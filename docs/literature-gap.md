# Literature Gap — Working Map

This document tracks *working gap hypotheses*. It is deliberately conservative: a gap is not treated as established novelty until it survives a broader literature and patent search.

## What is already well represented

Recent literature shows that the following ideas already exist in multiple forms:

- pH-responsive corrosion indicators;
- metal-ion-responsive colorimetric and fluorescent indicators;
- mechanically triggered damage-reporting microcapsules;
- coatings that combine self-reporting and self-healing;
- visual tracking of a repair process;
- multiple colors for different damage states;
- weathering-resistant self-warning/self-healing systems;
- quantitative optical/image analysis of coating degradation;
- material-memory concepts in adjacent mechanochromic coatings.

Therefore SENTINEL should not claim novelty merely from combining more functions.

## Gap cluster A — Diagnostic reliability under confounders

A recurring weakness is that sensing is often demonstrated under controlled conditions with a limited set of interfering variables. For marine use, pH-based sensing can be especially problematic because seawater itself is mildly alkaline.

Key research questions:

- What is the false-positive rate under benign humidity, salinity, temperature and pH variation?
- Does UV or thermal aging change the baseline optical signal?
- How does harmless coating damage differ from electrochemically active corrosion?
- Can a diagnostic channel remain selective after prolonged immersion or weathering?

**Current priority: highest.**

## Gap cluster B — Optical signal versus independent ground truth

A visible response is not automatically a quantitative corrosion measurement.

A stronger diagnostic system should relate optical observables to independent physical measurements such as EIS, polarization, mass loss, adhesion loss or verified corrosion-product formation.

Potential outputs:

- optical calibration curves;
- sensitivity/specificity and false-positive statistics;
- uncertainty bounds;
- severity estimates rather than simple alarm/no-alarm output.

**Current priority: highest.**

## Gap cluster C — Persistent material history

Material-memory and time-integration concepts are already demonstrated in mechanochromic polymers. The more specific question for SENTINEL is whether a protective interface can retain a useful history of a chemically or electrochemically critical event after the immediate environment returns toward normal.

Possible research targets include:

- threshold-event memory;
- cumulative exposure or dose integration;
- persistent evidence of past electrolyte ingress or corrosion activity;
- current-state and historical-state channels that remain distinguishable.

This is a high-upside direction but requires careful novelty and chemistry screening.

## Gap cluster D — Self-healing verification

Self-healing and visual repair tracking are already established concepts. The remaining useful question is whether a diagnostic signal can reliably indicate *functional* recovery rather than merely visible crack closure.

A strong validation scheme would compare:

- optical recovery state;
- EIS/barrier recovery;
- adhesion or permeability recovery;
- repeatability after multiple damage/healing cycles.

The key distinction is **closure versus restored protection**.

## Gap cluster E — Multi-stressor discrimination

Real infrastructure rarely experiences one isolated stressor. Marine and offshore surfaces may experience combinations of chloride, humidity, wet/dry cycling, UV, temperature variation, abrasion and biofouling.

Long-term SENTINEL work should test whether multiple degradation mechanisms can be distinguished without excessive channel cross-talk.

## Gap cluster F — Laboratory-to-field transfer

Accelerated corrosion tests are useful but do not always reproduce field ranking or degradation mechanisms. This creates a later-stage opportunity to compare controlled SENTINEL diagnostics with real outdoor/coastal exposure.

This is important but not the first undergraduate-scale experiment.

## First paper direction

The strongest accessible first publication hypothesis is currently:

**A quantitative cross-sensitivity and false-positive benchmark for optical corrosion-reporting coatings under marine-relevant environmental confounders, calibrated against independent electrochemical ground truth.**

This direction can produce useful science even before a highly novel coating chemistry is developed.

## Evidence policy

Every proposed gap will be challenged against:

1. peer-reviewed literature;
2. reviews and highly cited benchmark papers;
3. recent papers from the last 2–3 years;
4. patents before any strong novelty claim;
5. negative or contradictory evidence.

The project will prefer a smaller defensible claim over an unsupported “world first” claim.
