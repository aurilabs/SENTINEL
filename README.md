# SENTINEL

**Multistate Diagnostic Protective Interfaces for Harsh Environments**

SENTINEL is an independent materials-science research program focused on protective interfaces that do more than act as passive barriers. The long-term objective is to develop coatings and interfacial systems that can distinguish degradation states, quantify severity, preserve evidence of critical past exposure, and verify recovery after repair or self-healing.

## Current research question

> Can a protective coating distinguish true corrosion initiation from benign coating damage and environmental confounders with a quantitatively calibrated, low-false-positive optical response?

The first experimental envelope is carbon-steel infrastructure under marine/offshore-relevant exposure conditions. The research begins with controlled laboratory states and reproducible optical analysis before expanding to harsher environments.

## Research principles

- Mechanism before complexity.
- Independent ground truth before claims of sensing accuracy.
- False positives and cross-sensitivity are treated as primary design variables.
- Protection performance must not be sacrificed simply to add sensing functions.
- Negative results and failed hypotheses are retained as part of the research record.
- Potentially patentable formulations and unvalidated material architectures are not published prematurely.

## Program structure

- **Phase 0 — Literature and mechanism mapping**
- **Phase 1 — Robust diagnostic discrimination**
- **Phase 2 — Persistent damage memory**
- **Phase 3 — Self-healing verification**
- **Phase 4 — Multi-stressor discrimination**
- **Phase 5 — Harsh-environment branches**

Planned branches include `SENTINEL-Marine`, `SENTINEL-GEO`, and later laboratory-supported work relevant to hydrogen infrastructure.

## Repository scope

This repository will contain public, reproducible parts of the program: analysis code, calibration tools, selected protocols, processed/open datasets, figures, model documentation, and research reports. Early-stage formulation details or potentially patentable architectures may remain private until an appropriate disclosure decision is made.

## Status

**Active research — Phase 0**

The immediate work is a structured literature-gap map and an optical quantification toolkit for calibrated image analysis.
